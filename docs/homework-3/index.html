01 Stochastic models and foundations

The assignment models n systems as n penetration steps per attacker, and m attackers as m sample trajectories. For attacker a = 1,…,m and system i = 1,…,n, Xa,i is a score increment. Assume all increments are independent and identically distributed across both indices. Then Sa,k = Σi=1k Xa,i. Each attacker supplies three final outcomes: Sa,n, Sa,n/n, and Sa,n/√n. Their empirical distributions use all m attackers, from m × n sampled increments.

Bernoulli(p): X is 1 with probability p and 0 otherwise; one denotes a modeled successful penetration event, zero no event at that step. The total is nonnegative, Sₙ ∼ Binomial(n,p), with μ = p and σ² = p(1−p).

Rademacher: X is +1 or −1 with equal probabilities. Positive increments add progress and negative ones offset it. This is an abstract signed score; a negative score is not a negative number of compromised systems. Here μ = 0, σ² = 1 and Sₙ = 2Bₙ − n, where Bₙ ∼ Binomial(n,½). Possible totals are −n, −n+2,…,n, so parity matters.

Exact moments before simulation

Write μ = E[X] and σ² = Var(X). Linearity gives E[Sₙ] = nμ; independence makes the increment variances add, giving Var(Sₙ) = nσ². Applying E[cY] = cE[Y] and Var(cY) = c²Var(Y) gives:

04 LLN, CLT and conclusions

LLN: concentration of attacker averages

For every fixed ε > 0, P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) → 0: the average converges in probability to μ. Its empirical estimate counts the attackers outside that band and divides by m. Chebyshev gives the theoretical upper bound min(1, σ²/(nε²)), which tends to zero; it is not an estimate of the actual probability.

Solid points: empirical fraction. Dashed: theoretical bound at the grid values; connecting segments only guide the eye. Monte Carlo fluctuations can place an estimate above the true probability or its bound. Zero observed deviations means none in this finite sample, not that the true probability is zero.

CLT: a limiting distribution, not a limiting path

For Rademacher increments, Zₙ = Sₙ/√n ⇒ N(0,1), with exact E[Zₙ] = 0 and Var(Zₙ) = 1 for every n. The standard-normal density is φ(z) = exp(−z²/2)/√(2π). The sample mean collapses toward zero while Zₙ retains order-one fluctuations; convergence in distribution does not mean an individual normalized trajectory settles at a constant.

Bars: empirical density. Dashed curve: N(0,1) density in the same units. All three panels share an x-axis range. At small n, each bin covers one attainable score, spaced 2/√n apart; parity restricts the support. These finite discrete distributions are not exactly normal.

D = supz|F̂m,n(z) − Φ(z)| checks both sides of every empirical CDF jump. It is descriptive, not a hypothesis test or p-value. The approximate normal CDF uses A&S 26.2.17 (absolute error below 7.5 × 10−8). Lattice effects and Monte Carlo error remain; D need not improve monotonically.

Standard-normal overlays are disabled for Bernoulli’s uncentered scores. Select Rademacher and run again for the CLT comparison.

For fixed Bernoulli p > 0, E[Sₙ/√n] = p√n, so the uncentered sequence does not approach N(0,1). At p = 0 it is identically zero; at p = 1 it is the deterministic value √n. Only for 0 < p < 1 does the centered, standardized expression (Sₙ−np)/√(np(1−p)) ⇒ N(0,1). This optional formula does not replace the three quantities studied above. See MIT 18.05, reading 6b.

Systems, attackers, and the combined score

Increasing n lengthens each attacker’s process and changes its distribution. Increasing m gives more independent trajectories under the model, improving estimation precision without changing the one-attacker law. Separately, T(n,m) = Σa=1mSa,n combines all attackers: E[T] = mnμ, Var(T) = mnσ². Thus Bernoulli E[T] = mnp, while Rademacher E[T] = 0; aggregate variance grows with the modeled number of interactions when σ² > 0.

Conclusions and limitations

The Bernoulli total grows on average as np; the Rademacher total stays centered at zero with fluctuations of order √n. Both averages concentrate near their increment mean with variance proportional to 1/n. Dividing the total by √n stabilizes variance, but a standard-normal limit requires the appropriate centering and scale. Finite simulations illustrate the theorems rather than prove convergence.

The independent, identical increment model omits correlated attacks on shared systems, unequal vulnerabilities, adaptation, and nonstationarity. The seeded generator has a finite period and deterministic structure. These restrictions make a reproducible probability experiment, not a validated prediction of real compromises.
