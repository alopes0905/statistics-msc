Sampling Mean and Variance

Repeated penetration-score samples separate what an estimator targets from how much it varies: sample variances stabilize around 10, while their sampling variability tends to zero.

01 Model and theoretical moments

I assign each penetration attempt a score X ∈ {0,1,…,10} with P(X=k) = 1/11. This fixed discrete uniform model is educational, not calibrated vulnerability data. For hacker a = 1,…,m and system i = 1,…,n, assume all Xa,i are independent and identically distributed.

E[X] = (0+⋯+10)/11 = 5;
E[X²] = (0²+⋯+10²)/11 = 35;
σ² = Var(X) = 35−5² = 10.

Each hacker supplies a trajectory of n scores and two estimates, calculated by the shared Welford recurrence:

x̄ₐ = (1/n)Σᵢ Xₐ,ᵢ;
sₐ² = Σᵢ(Xₐ,ᵢ−x̄ₐ)²/(n−1), n ≥ 2.

Across hackers, meanOfMeans = (1/m)Σₐ x̄ₐ and meanOfVariances = (1/m)Σₐ sₐ². The latter averages within-hacker variances; it is not the variance of the means.

Why sample variances also concentrate

The fourth central moment is μ₄ = (1/11)Σₖ₌₀¹⁰(k−5)⁴ = 2(1+16+81+256+625)/11 = 178. Using the general iid formula (PDG, equation 40.7), with σ⁴ = (σ²)² = 100:

Var(sₐ²) = [μ₄ − ((n−3)/(n−1))σ⁴]/n
= [78 + 200/(n−1)]/n → 0.

E[sₐ²] = 10 at every n ≥ 2; Var(sₐ²) tends to zero. These are different claims. Also, independence across hackers gives Var(meanOfMeans) = 10/(mn) and Var(meanOfVariances) = Var(sₐ²)/m. Their standard errors are the square roots of these quantities.

02 Simulation and Welford estimators

Each hacker’s scores are generated once. Smaller n checkpoints reuse prefixes of that same trajectory. The grid is 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 500, 1,000, up to the selected maximum. All m hackers enter every statistic; only the first 20 paths are drawn.

Reproducible experiment

Integer seed 1–2,147,483,646. At most 1,000 hackers × 1,000 systems = 1,000,000 scores; calculated in a cancellable worker.

JavaScript and Web Workers are required to generate empirical results.

Solid paths: individual running estimates; dashed line: target 5 or 10. Variance begins at k = 2. At most about 230 points per displayed path; all scores are processed.

The Park–Miller PRNG uses state ← 16807 × state mod (2³¹−1). Exact jump-ahead allocates a fixed, disjoint block of 1,000 draws per hacker, preserving paths when m or n changes. Its 2³¹−2 states split into eleven equally sized integer buckets for scores 0–10. Deterministic streams approximate iid sampling; disjoint blocks do not prove independence. This is simulation randomness, not cryptographic randomness.

04 Inferential interpretation and conclusions

Unbiasedness: E[x̄ₐ] = 5 and E[sₐ²] = 10. Indeed, SS = Σᵢ(Xᵢ−μ)² − n(x̄−μ)², so E[SS] = nσ² − n(σ²/n) = (n−1)σ². Dividing by n−1 removes the finite-n downward bias; dividing by n instead would give expected value 10(n−1)/n.

Increasing n: both estimator variances tend to zero. Chebyshev’s inequality therefore gives convergence in probability of x̄ₐ to 5 and sₐ² to 10: both are consistent. Sample variances become more stable around 10, not around zero. The individual-score variance stays 10 throughout.

Increasing m: the across-hacker averages become more precise, with variances reduced by a factor m. It does not change the theoretical sampling distribution for one hacker at fixed n. Finite runs need not improve monotonically, and checkpoints are dependent because they reuse prefixes.

Inference: in applications, sample statistics estimate unknown population parameters. Unbiasedness concerns their expected targets; consistency concerns their large-sample limits; sampling variability quantifies precision. Standard errors, confidence intervals and other inferential procedures use this distinction. The PDG review, §40.2 formalizes these properties. These experiments illustrate the theory; they do not prove it.

Limits: real penetration outcomes can be correlated, heterogeneous or adaptive. Then the iid formulas need not hold. The bounded uniform score model, finite m and deterministic finite-period PRNG constrain this experiment; the evidence concerns this specified educational model.
