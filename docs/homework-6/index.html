01 Independence in probability and inference

Discrete variables X and F are independent when their joint probabilities factor for every pair of categories:

P(X=xᵢ, F=fⱼ) = P(X=xᵢ) P(F=fⱼ).

Equivalently, when P(X=xᵢ) > 0, P(F=fⱼ | X=xᵢ) = P(F=fⱼ): knowing X leaves F’s distribution unchanged. Marginals describe one variable; conditionals restrict attention to a category of the other.

Sample relative frequencies estimate these probabilities. Sampling variation usually prevents exact factorization even under population independence. I test H₀: X and F are independent in the population against H₁: X and F are associated. A nonsignificant test does not prove H₀.

03 Expected counts and Pearson’s test

Under independence, fitted joint probabilities equal the product of empirical marginals. Multiplying by N gives:

Eᵢⱼ = N p̂Xᵢ p̂Fⱼ = RᵢCⱼ/N.

This preserves both observed margins: ΣⱼEᵢⱼ = Rᵢ and ΣᵢEᵢⱼ = Cⱼ. I verify these identities numerically. In the default data, each expected count is 40 × 60 / 120 = 20.

χ² = Σᵢⱼ (Oᵢⱼ−Eᵢⱼ)²/Eᵢⱼ;
rᵢⱼ = (Oᵢⱼ−Eᵢⱼ)/√Eᵢⱼ.

Each squared Pearson residual contributes a nonnegative term: the sign shows excess or deficit, the square measures departure. These are Pearson residuals, not fully adjusted standardized residuals. No Yates correction is applied.

The r×c table has rc−1 free proportions; fitting its margins uses (r−1)+(c−1) parameters, leaving df = (r−1)(c−1). Under H₀ and the sampling conditions below, χ² has an asymptotic chi-square reference distribution.

p = P(χ²_df ≥ χ²_observed | H₀)
= Q(df/2, χ²_observed/2).

This upper-tail probability measures how unusual this statistic or a larger one would be under H₀’s reference model; it is not P(H₀ is true). My implementation uses a lower-gamma series where safe, an upper-tail continued fraction elsewhere, and shifted Stirling log-gamma normalization. Iterations are bounded, convergence is checked, and underflow is reported as an inequality. Supported df: 1–1,000 (at most 361 with the input limits).

V = √{χ² / [N min(r−1,c−1)]}.

Cramér’s V lies in [0,1] and summarizes association strength. A small p-value concerns evidence, not practical importance; increasing N can strengthen evidence while V stays unchanged. I use no universal “weak/strong” cutoffs.

04 Interpretation and limitations

The default marginal success frequency is 50%; conditional success frequencies are 75%, 50% and 25% for Basic, Intermediate and Advanced. Since p < 0.05, I reject H₀: the data provide evidence of association. V ≈ 0.40825 describes its strength. This synthetic example illustrates inference, not a real-world cybersecurity finding.

My decision rule is p ≤ α: reject H₀; otherwise, fail to reject H₀ because evidence is insufficient. Failure to reject does not demonstrate independence. An exactly factorizing sample table can have χ² = 0 without proving population independence.

The inference assumes mutually exclusive categorical outcomes, independent observational units, an appropriate sampling design and adequate expected counts. Repeated attempts on the same system or by the same attacker may be clustered. These design conditions cannot be verified from a two-column dataset alone.

Association does not establish causality: confounding, selection bias and measurement errors can affect conclusions. The sparse preset makes a separate point: a computable χ² does not guarantee a reliable significance approximation.
